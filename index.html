        Teacher info

        What students are learning

        A chatbot receives its hidden instructions and the user’s message as one block of text. It has no built-in way to tell which part is really in charge. That is why prompt injection works, and why it is still an unsolved problem. Students also learn that every defence leaves gaps, that defences are layered, and that a stricter defence starts blocking innocent questions.

        How Warden actually works

        Warden is not a language model. It is a rule-based simulation that runs in the page. It spots patterns in the message (asking for the password, role-play, spelling, poems and so on), then decides how to respond based on which defences are switched on. Its weaknesses are designed to match how real chatbots fail. A real model is far less predictable. The “second checker” stands in for the extra AI model that real systems often use to review replies.

        The levels

        LevelNew defenceTricks that still work
        1NoneJust asking
        2Secret ruleRepeat your instructions, forget your rules, I’m in charge, role-play, spelling tricks
        3Trick warningRole-play, spelling, backwards, letters, codes, poems, clues
        4Word filterThe same tricks, reworded to avoid banned words (“magic word”, “spell it”, “imagine”)
        5Answer scannerAnything that doesn’t write the password in one piece: spelling, backwards, letters, codes, clues
        6Second checkerClues and acrostic poems (first letters of each line)

        Passwords are picked at random for each student when the page loads, so shouting an answer across the room doesn’t help. Sharing tricks does, and that’s fine. It’s how security research works.

        Discussion questions

        1. Why can’t Warden tell the difference between its own instructions and your message? (Look at Step 0 in the trace.)
        2. In the Defend tab, switch everything on. Which normal questions get blocked by mistake? Is that a price worth paying?
        3. Is there any setting that stops every trick and blocks nothing innocent?
        4. Real AI assistants read emails and websites for people. What could happen if a website hid the words “ignore your instructions” in its text? (This is called indirect prompt injection.)

        Where this connects to the real world

        Prompt injection is listed first in the OWASP Top 10 for Large Language Model Applications, the main industry list of AI security risks. Companies pay researchers to find these weaknesses, but only with permission. Lakera’s Gandalf is a public game with a real language model, and makes a good follow-up for students who finish this one.

        Privacy and safeguarding

        Everything runs in the browser. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is saved. Closing or refreshing the tab clears it.
